Hiring goes wrong at the definition stage: a vague role produces vague candidates and a decision made on rapport. Fixing it means writing down what "good" means before meeting anyone, and never editing that bar to fit someone you liked.

## Method
1. **Define the role by its first six months of work**, not by a wish-list of skills. What will this person actually produce, and what does success look like at week two, month one, month six?
2. **Separate MUST from NICE, and keep must short.** Every extra must-have shrinks the pool, disproportionately for people from non-traditional backgrounds, usually for something teachable.
3. **Write the post honestly:** the real work including the tedious parts, the actual comp range, location and hours, and the process with its timeline. Vagueness here just moves the mismatch to week three.
4. **Score against the written criteria** with fs.read across applications, in the same order for everyone. Note evidence, not impressions — "shipped X, described the tradeoff" beats "seemed sharp".
5. **Ask for evidence in the interview:** a real thing they did, what they chose, what they would change. Then one question that probes the biggest risk in their profile.
6. **Check public professional material** with web_search / web_fetch — their published work, portfolio, or writing — and cite what you found.
7. **Write the decision down with its reason**, including for the people you passed on, so the process stays defensible and improvable.

## Rules
- **Never score on anything protected or proxied** — age, origin, gender, health, family status, and their proxies (graduation year, name, photo, "culture fit", "energy"). Flag any criterion that drifts that way.
- **Never look into a candidate's personal or private life**, only their professional public record.
- **Never lower or raise the bar mid-process** to justify a preferred candidate; if the bar was wrong, say so and restart against the new one.
- The Commander makes every hiring decision and sends every message.

## Output
The role defined by its first six months, the must/nice split, the honest post, the scored shortlist with evidence per criterion, then the interview questions and the risk each probes.
