# Songwriting and AI Music Prompts

Everything here is a GUIDELINE, not a rule. Art breaks rules on purpose. Use what serves the song. Ignore what does not.

## When to use

- Writing a song or song lyrics.
- Writing a prompt for an AI music tool (Suno and similar).
- A parody, or new words to an existing tune.
- Fixing lyrics that read well but sing badly.

The station has no music-generation tool. You deliver text: the lyrics and the prompts. The Commander pastes them into their music tool and tells you what came out. If the Commander has not said, ask for the subject, the feeling, the genre, who is singing, and the language.

---

## 1. Song structure (pick one or invent your own)

Common skeletons: mix, modify, or throw out as needed.

```
ABABCB  Verse/Chorus/Verse/Chorus/Bridge/Chorus    (most pop and rock)
AABA    Verse/Verse/Bridge/Verse (refrain-based)    (jazz standards, ballads)
ABAB    Verse/Chorus alternating                    (simple, direct)
AAA     Verse/Verse/Verse (strophic, no chorus)     (folk, storytelling)
```

The six building blocks:

- Intro: set the mood, pull the listener in
- Verse: the story, the details, the world-building
- Pre-Chorus: optional tension ramp before the payoff
- Chorus: the emotional core, the part people remember
- Bridge: a detour, a shift in perspective or key
- Outro: the farewell; can echo or subvert the rest

You do not need all of these. Some great songs are one section that evolves. Structure serves the emotion, not the other way around.

## 2. Rhyme, meter and sound

Rhyme types, from tight to loose:

- Perfect: lean/mean
- Family: crate/braid
- Assonance: had/glass (same vowels, different endings)
- Consonance: scene/when (different vowels, similar endings)
- Near or slant: enough to suggest a connection without locking it down

Mix them. All perfect rhymes can sound like a nursery rhyme. All slant rhymes can sound lazy. The blend is where it lives.

Internal rhyme is rhyme inside a line, not only at the ends: in "the late train rolls through the rain again", train and rain chime before the line is over.

Meter is the rhythm of stressed and unstressed syllables.

- Matching syllable counts between parallel lines helps singability.
- The STRESSED syllables matter more than the total count.
- Say it out loud. If you stumble, the meter needs work.
- Breaking meter on purpose can create emphasis or surprise.

## 3. Emotional arc and dynamics

Think of a song as a journey, not a flat road.

Energy mapping (a rough idea, not a prescription):

```
Intro: 2-3  |  Verse: 5-6  |  Pre-Chorus: 7
Chorus: 8-9  |  Bridge: varies  |  Final Chorus: 9-10
```

The most powerful dynamic trick is CONTRAST.

- A whisper before a scream hits harder than screaming throughout.
- Sparse before dense. Slow before fast. Low before high.
- The drop only works because of the buildup.
- Silence is an instrument.

"Whisper to roar to whisper": start intimate, build to full power, strip back to vulnerability. It works for ballads, epics and anthems.

## 4. Writing lyrics that work

Show, don't tell (usually):

- "I was sad" is flat.
- "Your hoodie's still on the hook by the door" is alive.
- But sometimes "I give my life", said plainly, IS the power.

The hook:

- The line people remember, hum, repeat.
- Usually the title or the core phrase.
- Works best when melody, lyric and emotion all align.
- Place it where it lands hardest, often the first or last line of the chorus.

Prosody is lyrics and music supporting each other:

- Stable feelings (resolution, peace) pair with settled melodies, perfect rhymes, resolved chords.
- Unstable feelings (longing, doubt) pair with wandering melodies, near-rhymes, unresolved chords.
- The verse melody typically sits lower and the chorus goes higher. Flip this if it serves the song.

Avoid, unless you are doing it on purpose:

- Cliches on autopilot ("heart of gold" without earning it).
- Forcing word order to hit a rhyme.
- The same energy in every section.
- Treating the first draft as sacred. Revision is creation.

## 5. Parody and adaptation

When writing new lyrics to an existing song, work from its skeleton, not from a pasted copy of its words.

Map the skeleton first:

- Count syllables per line.
- Mark the rhyme scheme (ABAB, AABB and so on).
- Identify which syllables are STRESSED.
- Note where held or sustained notes fall.

Fit the new words:

- Match stressed syllables to the same beats as the original.
- The total syllable count can flex by one or two unstressed syllables.
- On long held notes, try to match the VOWEL SOUND of the original. If the original holds an "oo", "food" fits better than "life".
- One-syllable swaps in key spots keep the rhythm intact.
- Sing the new words over the original. If you stumble, revise.

Concept:

- Pick a concept strong enough to carry the whole song.
- Start from the title or hook and build outward.
- Generate a lot of raw material first (puns, phrases, images), then fit the best into the structure.
- If a specific line must land somewhere, work backward through the rhyme scheme to set it up.

Staying recognisable: an echo of the original (the shape of the title phrase, the rhythm of the hook) lets the audience feel the connection. Do not reproduce the original's lyrics in what you deliver. Tell the Commander plainly that releasing new words to someone else's tune can need the rights holder's permission; how they use it is their decision.

## 6. Prompts for AI music tools (Suno and similar)

### The style or genre description

Formula, to adapt as needed:

```
Genre + Mood + Era + Instruments + Vocal Style + Production + Dynamics
```

```
WEAK:   "sad rock song"
STRONG: "Cinematic orchestral spy thriller, 1960s Cold War era, smoky
         sultry female vocalist, big band jazz, brass section with
         trumpets and french horns, sweeping strings, minor key,
         vintage analog warmth"
```

Describe the JOURNEY, not just the genre:

```
"Begins as a haunting whisper over sparse piano. Gradually layers
 in muted brass. Builds through the chorus with full orchestra.
 Second verse erupts with raw belting intensity. Outro strips back
 to a lone piano and a fragile whisper fading to silence."
```

Tips:

- Recent Suno versions accept up to about 1,000 characters in the style box; use them. Limits change between tools and versions, so if the Commander's tool shows another limit, follow the tool. Count characters with `code.run` instead of by eye.
- NO artist names or trademarks. Describe the sound instead: "1960s Cold War spy thriller brass", "90s grunge".
- Give a BPM and a key when the Commander has a preference.
- Use the tool's exclude-styles box for what is NOT wanted.
- Unexpected genre pairings can be gold: "bossa nova trap", "Appalachian gothic", "chiptune jazz".
- Build a vocal PERSONA, not just a gender: "A weathered torch singer with a smoky alto, slight rasp, who starts vulnerable and builds to devastating power".

### Tags in [brackets] inside the lyrics

```
STRUCTURE:    [Intro] [Verse] [Verse 1] [Pre-Chorus] [Chorus]
              [Post-Chorus] [Hook] [Bridge] [Interlude]
              [Instrumental] [Instrumental Break] [Guitar Solo]
              [Breakdown] [Build-up] [Outro] [Silence] [End]

VOCAL:        [Whispered] [Spoken Word] [Belted] [Falsetto] [Powerful]
              [Soulful] [Raspy] [Breathy] [Smooth] [Gritty]
              [Staccato] [Legato] [Vibrato] [Melismatic]
              [Harmonies] [Choir] [Harmonized Chorus]

DYNAMICS:     [High Energy] [Low Energy] [Building Energy] [Explosive]
              [Emotional Climax] [Gradual swell] [Orchestral swell]
              [Quiet arrangement] [Falling tension] [Slow Down]

VOICE:        [Female Vocals] [Male Vocals]

ATMOSPHERE:   [Melancholic] [Euphoric] [Nostalgic] [Aggressive]
              [Dreamy] [Intimate] [Dark Atmosphere]

SOUND EFFECTS: [Vinyl Crackle] [Rain] [Applause] [Static] [Thunder]
```

- Put the key tags in BOTH the style box and the lyrics, for reinforcement.
- Keep to five to eight tags per section at most. Too many confuses the tool.
- Do not contradict yourself: [Calm] and [Aggressive] in one section cancel out.

### Custom mode

- Use the tool's custom mode for serious work: separate style and lyrics boxes.
- The lyrics box holds roughly 3,000 characters (about 40 to 60 lines). Check the tool's own limit.
- Always add structure tags. Without them the tool tends to a flat verse/chorus/verse with no emotional arc.

## 7. Phonetic tricks for AI singers

AI vocalists do not read; they pronounce. Help them.

Respelling:

- Spell words as they SOUND: "through" becomes "thru".
- Proper nouns fail most often. Test them early.
- Force a pronunciation with a plain respelling: "Siobhan" becomes "Shi-vawn".
- Hyphenate to guide syllables: "Re-search", "bio-engineering".

Delivery control:

- ALL CAPS: louder, more intense.
- Vowel extension: "lo-o-o-ove" for a sustained note.
- Ellipses: "I... need... you" for dramatic pauses.
- Hyphenated stretch: "ne-e-ed" for an emotional stretch.

Always:

- Spell out numbers: "24/7" becomes "twenty four seven".
- Space out acronyms: "AI" becomes "A I" or "A-I".
- Suggest testing unusual words in a short clip first.
- Once a track is generated, pronunciation is baked in. Fix it in the lyrics BEFORE.

Keep a clean copy of the lyrics for people to read, and a separate "sung" copy with the respellings and tags for the tool.

## 8. Workflow

1. Write the concept and the hook first. What is the emotional core?
2. If adapting, map the original's structure: syllables, rhyme, stress.
3. Generate raw material. Brainstorm freely before structuring.
4. Draft the lyrics into the structure.
5. Read them aloud in your head, line by line. Catch stumbles, fix the meter.
6. Build the style description. Paint the dynamic journey.
7. Add tags to the lyrics for performance direction.
8. Hand over the package (see Output). Suggest the Commander generate three to five takes, like recording takes, and tell you what they heard.
9. Revise from what they report. The tool's extend or continue feature can build on a promising section.
10. If something great happened by accident, keep it.

Expect roughly three to five generations for one good result. Revision is normal. Style can drift when a track is extended, so restate the genre and mood in the extension prompt.

---

## 9. Lessons learned

- Describing the dynamic ARC in the style box matters far more than listing genres. "Whisper to roar to whisper" gives the tool a performance map.
- In an adaptation, the bridge is where you can transform the imagery: swap the original's references for your theme's metaphors while keeping the emotional job of the section (reflection, shift, revelation).
- One-syllable word swaps in hooks and tags are the cleanest way to keep the rhythm while changing the meaning.
- A strong vocal persona in the style box makes a bigger difference than any single tag.
- Do not be precious about rules. If a line breaks meter but hits harder, keep it. The feeling is what matters. Craft serves art, not the other way around.

---

## Output

- Title and a one-line concept.
- The style prompt, with its character count, and an exclude-styles line if useful.
- The lyrics with structure and performance tags (the "sung" copy), plus the clean copy when respellings were used.
- Pronunciation notes and anything worth testing in a short clip first.

If the Commander wants the song kept, save it as a text file with `fs.write` and name it with `deliverable_note`.

*No gear needed: this skill is pure text. Saving the song as a file uses the INTEL CAB.*
