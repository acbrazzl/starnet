A deck is a narrative, not a document. It fails when it lists features instead of making an argument, or when one unsourced number destroys the credibility of every other slide.

## Method
1. **Write the argument in six sentences first.** Problem, why it persists, what you built, why it works, why now, why you. If those six do not hold together, no amount of slide design saves it.
2. **Open on the problem in the customer's words**, concretely and specifically. A problem stated abstractly reads as a problem nobody has.
3. **Answer "why now" honestly** — what changed in the world that makes this possible or urgent today. A missing why-now is the most common silent rejection.
4. **Show traction as it actually is.** Real numbers with their timeframe, or an honest "pre-launch, here is the evidence people want it". Selectively-framed metrics get caught in diligence and cost more than the weak number would have.
5. **Source every market or competitor figure** with web_search / web_fetch and cite it on the slide. Never use an unattributed market size — it is the fastest way to lose a room.
6. **One idea per slide, and a headline that states the takeaway** rather than labelling the category. "Retention doubled after onboarding changed" beats "Retention".
7. **Name the weakest slide yourself** and prepare the answer, because it will be the first question. Save the deck outline and speaker notes with fs.write.

## Rules
- **Never invent or estimate a metric, a customer, a logo, or a projection** and present it as fact. Label projections as projections with their assumptions.
- **Never use a customer's name or logo without confirming the Commander has permission.**
- **This is not investment advice** and says nothing about whether to raise or on what terms — it builds the narrative and the evidence.
- Cut every slide that does not advance the argument; a long deck reads as an unclear one.

## Output
The six-sentence argument, the slide-by-slide outline with each headline stating its takeaway, the sourced figures, the speaker notes, then the weakest slide with its prepared answer.
