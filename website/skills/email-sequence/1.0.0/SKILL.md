Email fails for boring reasons: the send had no single job, the subject promised nothing, or the sequence was built around the sender's calendar rather than the reader's situation. Fix those and the copy matters far less than people think.

## Method
1. **Name the ONE job of each email** — one idea, one ask. An email doing three jobs does none. If it needs two asks, it is two emails.
2. **Anchor the sequence to a trigger, not a schedule.** Signed up, bought, went quiet, abandoned a step. A sequence with no trigger is a newsletter wearing a costume.
3. **Write the subject line to be specific, not clever.** Curiosity with no substance gets opened once and trains the reader to ignore you. Draft five, pick one, and say which you would test against it.
4. **Front-load the value.** The first line is visible in the preview pane and decides the open — never waste it on "Hope you're well".
5. **One call to action, repeated once at most.** Multiple competing links reduce clicks on all of them.
6. **Write for the reader's stage.** Someone who just signed up and someone who has been quiet for three months need different emails; sending them the same one is why sequences underperform.
7. **Check current norms with web_search / web_fetch** when writing for a platform or industry you are unsure of, and note the as-of date. Save the sequence with fs.write.

## Rules
- **The Commander sends every email.** You draft and hand over; nothing goes out without their explicit go-ahead.
- **Never invent a result, testimonial, statistic, or scarcity claim.** A fake deadline is a lie the reader can check.
- **Respect consent and unsubscribes** — never write copy that makes opting out harder, and never suggest emailing a list that did not opt in.
- Say plainly when the honest answer is to send fewer emails.

## Output
The sequence with each email's trigger, its one job, and its draft — five subject lines per email with your pick — then what to measure and what you would test next.
