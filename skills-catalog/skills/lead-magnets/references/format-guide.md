# Format guide

How to build each lead magnet format. You can draft the text of any of these as a plain file with `fs.write`. Layout, design, quiz tools, video and webinar hosting are the Commander's; skip those parts unless the Commander has the tool, and hand over text they can drop into whatever they use.

## Guides and ebooks

Best for: authority, deep education, early-stage leads.

Structure:

1. Title page.
2. Contents.
3. Introduction: frame the problem, set expectations.
4. Three to seven chapters, one key idea each.
5. Summary of the takeaways.
6. Next step toward the product.

Guidelines: 10 to 25 pages, shorter if it stays valuable. Note where a chart, diagram or screenshot belongs. Pull out key numbers or quotes. End each chapter with a one-line takeaway. Density beats length: do not pad.

## Checklists

Best for: processes, quick wins, help with implementation.

Structure: a title in the form "[Number]-point [topic] checklist"; numbered or tick-box items; sections if there are more than ten items; one or two sentences of explanation per item.

Guidelines: one to two pages. Action wording ("Verify X", "Set up Y", "Remove Z"). Order by workflow or by priority. Printable: clean layout, room to tick.

Works well for: step-by-step processes, audit criteria, launch lists, setup guides.

## Cheat sheets

Best for: reference, shortcuts, quick look-up.

Structure: one page, two at most; organised by category or workflow; dense but easy to scan, with clear headers and grouping.

Guidelines: built for looking things up, not for reading through. Tables, grids or columns. Formulas, shortcuts or snippets. The most important items in bold.

Works well for: keyboard shortcuts, formula references, glossaries, decision tables.

## Templates

Best for: repeatable processes, planning, tracking.

**Spreadsheet templates:** a "how to use" tab; example data filled in; dropdowns for fields with fixed choices; formula cells protected and input cells open. You can write the column layout, the example rows and the formulas as text or as a CSV file; the Commander builds the shared sheet.

**Document templates:** placeholder text in [brackets]; short instructions inline; offered in the formats the audience uses.

**Workspace-app templates** (Notion and similar): skip unless the Commander uses the app. If they do, give the page structure and example content as text for them to build and share.

Key principle: a template should be usable within five minutes of getting it.

## Swipe files

Best for: inspiration, learning from examples.

Structure: a curated set of 15 to 50 examples, organised by category or use. For each: the example (a link or a short excerpt), why it works in two or three points, and how to adapt it in a sentence or two.

Guidelines: quality over quantity. Add analysis; a bare collection is not worth an email. Credit every source, link to the original, and quote only short excerpts of other people's work. Gather examples with `web_search` and `web_fetch`, and include only ones you actually opened.

Works well for: subject lines, landing pages, ad copy, calls to action, onboarding flows, pricing pages.

## Mini-courses

**By email:** three to five emails over five to seven days. One lesson per email, one idea per lesson. Each email: teach, show an example, give an exercise. Lessons build on each other. The last email sums up and gives the next step. The `email-sequence` skill writes the emails; the Commander's email tool sends them.

**By video:** three to five videos of 5 to 15 minutes, with a worksheet per lesson, links delivered by email. You can write the scripts and worksheets; recording and hosting are the Commander's.

Cadence: every one to two days. Each lesson must be useful on its own: someone who sees only lesson 2 still learns something.

## Quizzes and assessments

Best for: engagement, segmenting, personalised results.

Questions: five to ten, seven is the sweet spot. Multiple choice only. They should feel insightful, not obvious. Show progress ("question 3 of 7").

Results: three to five result types. Each has a name, a description and tailored recommendations. Follow-up emails differ by result. A result people want to share helps.

Gate: the quiz is open; the personalised result asks for the email.

You can write the questions, the scoring rules and the result texts. Building the interactive quiz needs a quiz tool: skip unless the Commander has one.

## Webinars and workshops

**Live:** 30 to 45 minutes of teaching plus 15 minutes of questions. Shape: hook, three key points, a demonstration or example, the next step. Promote one to two weeks ahead. Three reminder emails: on registration, the day before, an hour before. Record it.

**On demand:** the same shape, edited tighter, always available behind an email registration, with an automatic follow-up.

Follow-up within 24 hours: the recording link, a summary and the next step.

You can write the outline, the talking points, the registration page text and the reminder and follow-up emails (as drafts). Hosting, recording and sending are the Commander's. There is no tool here to transcribe a recording: for a written summary, work from the speaker notes or a transcript the Commander provides.

Key principle: teach something truly useful. A webinar that is only a sales pitch costs trust.
