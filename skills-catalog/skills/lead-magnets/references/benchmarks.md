# Lead magnet benchmarks

**Read this first.** These are general ranges from the upstream author, given without sources. StarNet has not verified them. Real results depend on the audience, the niche, the traffic and the quality of the offer. Use the ranges to sanity-check a plan and to set a first target, label them as rough whenever you show them, and replace them with the Commander's own numbers as soon as those exist.

## Sign-up rate on a landing page

By format:

| Format | Rough range | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Checklist | 30 to 50% | Low commitment |
| Cheat sheet | 25 to 40% | Quick reference appeal |
| Template | 25 to 45% | Useful at once |
| Guide or ebook | 20 to 35% | More commitment |
| Quiz | 30 to 50% | Engagement carries people through |
| Webinar | 20 to 40% register | About 30 to 50% of those attend |
| Mini-course | 15 to 30% | More commitment, better leads |
| Free trial | 5 to 15% | High intent, high friction |

By traffic source:

| Source | Rough range | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Content upgrade inside a post | 3 to 8% of readers | Fits what they are reading |
| Dedicated page, search or direct visitors | 20 to 40% | High intent |
| Dedicated page, paid visitors | 10 to 25% | Cold traffic |
| Exit prompt | 2 to 5% of visitors | An interruption |
| Sidebar or banner box | 0.5 to 2% | Easy to ignore |
| Social link | 10 to 20% | Warm but browsing |

## Lead quality

Signs of good leads: they open the first three emails at a high rate; they click through to content or product pages; they come back to the site within a month; they match the ideal customer by role, company size and industry; they move to a trial, demo or purchase within about 90 days.

Signs of poor leads: they unsubscribe within the first three emails; they never open anything after the delivery email; they use throwaway addresses; they do not match the target customer; they wanted the content and have no interest in the product.

Volume against quality by format:

| Format | Volume | Quality |
|---|---|---|
| Generic ebook | High | Low to medium |
| Specific template | Medium | High |
| Industry report | Medium | Medium to high |
| Quiz or assessment | High | Medium, and easy to segment |
| Webinar | Low to medium | High |
| Checklist | High | Low to medium |
| Free trial | Low | Very high |

## Cost

Rough cost per lead by channel (US dollars):

| Channel | Range | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Search visitors | 0 to 5 | Lowest, slow to build |
| Content upgrade on the blog | 0 to 2 | Nearly free with existing traffic |
| Social lead ads | 3 to 15 | Consumer lower, business higher |
| Search ads | 10 to 50 | High intent, higher cost |
| LinkedIn ads | 25 to 75 | Business audiences; expensive, well qualified |
| Partner promotion | 0 to 5 | Depends on the relationship |

Creation cost if the Commander pays a designer or writer: a checklist, cheat sheet or template a few hundred dollars; a 10 to 25 page guide 500 to 2,000; an email course 500 to 1,500; a video course 2,000 to 5,000. Text you draft here covers most of the writing cost.

## Timelines

Time to create, for one person: checklist 1 to 2 hours; cheat sheet 2 to 4 hours; template 2 to 8 hours; swipe file 4 to 8 hours; guide 1 to 3 weeks; quiz 1 to 2 weeks; webinar 1 week of preparation; mini-course 1 to 2 weeks.

Time to results:

| Phase | Typical |
|---|---|
| First leads | At once, with existing traffic or paid promotion |
| Meaningful lead volume | 1 to 3 months |
| Search traffic growth | 2 to 6 months |
| Visible effect on sales | 3 to 6 months |
| A fair judgment of return | 6 to 12 months |

Start measuring on day one so the Commander builds benchmarks of their own.
