# Lead Magnets

Plan a lead magnet: a free resource people trade their email address for, which attracts the right people and leads naturally toward the product. The bar: one specific problem, one format, an outline the Commander can build from, and a promotion and measurement plan with honest numbers.

## When to use

- "What should I give away to get emails?" "Plan a checklist, template, guide or email course."
- "Turn this blog post into a download."
- An existing lead magnet that collects emails but not customers.

Not for the landing page copy itself (`landing-copy`), the emails that follow the sign-up (`email-sequence`), the publishing schedule (`content-calendar`), ad variations (`ad-copy-testing`), or a review of the finished capture page (`conversion-review`).

## Method

1. **Gather context.** Look for a product brief and existing content in the workspace with `fs.search`, then ask only for what is missing:
   - Business: what the company does, the ideal customer, the problems the product solves.
   - Today: how leads are captured now, what offers exist, the current sign-up rate.
   - Assets: posts, guides, data, internal templates and expertise that could be reused.
   - Goal: list growth, lead quality or product education; which buyer stage; time and budget.
2. **Pick the topic.** One specific problem, not a broad subject. A good source is the question prospects most often ask before buying. If the DISH is placed, check what competitors already offer (`web_search`, `web_fetch`) so the Commander's is not a copy of something freely available.
3. **Pick the format** by buyer stage and effort, using the tables below.
4. **Test it against the five principles.** Change the topic or format until all five hold.
5. **Outline it.** Use the structure for the chosen format in `references/format-guide.md` (included at the end of this skill). Give title options, every section with its key points, target length, what makes it worth having, and the next step toward the product at the end.
6. **Plan the exchange.** What is gated, which fields are asked for, how the offer is framed.
7. **Plan the page and delivery.** Landing page structure, delivery method, thank-you page.
8. **Plan promotion.** Which channels, in what order, with what the Commander already has.
9. **Plan measurement.** The numbers to watch and the first thing to test.
10. **Write the plan,** save it with `fs.write`, and name it with `deliverable_note`. Offer to draft the magnet itself next if it is a text format (checklist, cheat sheet, guide, template text, email course).

## Five principles

1. **Solves one specific problem.** "How to write cold emails that get replies" beats "Marketing guide".
2. **Matches the buyer stage.** People who are just becoming aware need education; people comparing options need help evaluating; people ready to decide need help implementing.
3. **High perceived value, low time cost.** It should look worth paying for, take under 30 minutes to use (under 10 is better), and give something to act on at once.
4. **A natural path to the product.** It solves a problem the product also solves, shows a gap the product fills, and demonstrates expertise.
5. **Easy to use.** One format, works on a phone, no special software.

## Formats

| Type | Best for | Effort | Time to create |
|---|---|---|---|
| Checklist | Quick wins, process steps | Low | 1 to 2 hours |
| Cheat sheet | Reference, shortcuts | Low | 2 to 4 hours |
| Template (document, spreadsheet) | Repeatable processes | Low to medium | 2 to 8 hours |
| Swipe file | Examples, inspiration | Medium | 4 to 8 hours |
| Guide or ebook | Deep education, authority | High | 1 to 3 weeks |
| Email mini-course | Education plus nurture | Medium | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Video mini-course | Education plus personality | High | 2 to 4 weeks |
| Quiz or assessment | Segmenting, engagement | Medium | 1 to 2 weeks |
| Webinar | Authority, live contact | Medium | 1 week of preparation |
| Resource library | Ongoing value | High | Ongoing |

You can draft the text of the first six. Design, quiz tools, video and webinar hosting are the Commander's: skip them unless the Commander has the tool, and hand over plain text they can lay out anywhere.

### By buyer stage

| Stage | Goal | Formats, with examples |
|---|---|---|
| Awareness | Teach the problem; reach people who do not know the company | Checklist ("10-point website audit"), cheat sheet, guide, quiz |
| Consideration | Help them weigh solutions; build trust | Comparison template, self-assessment, case study collection, webinar |
| Decision | Help them implement; remove friction | Ready-to-use templates, implementation guide ("switch in 30 minutes"), free trial |

## The exchange

| Approach | When | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| Fully gated | High-value, late-stage content | Most emails, least reach |
| Part gated | A preview free, the rest for an email | Balance |
| Open, email optional | Early-stage education | Most reach, fewest emails |
| Content upgrade | A bonus attached to a specific post | In context, high intent |

**What to ask for.** Email only converts best. Email and name allows personalisation. Adding company or role qualifies leads and costs sign-ups. Many fields only for high-value offers such as webinars and demos. Ask for the minimum: each extra field costs sign-ups (the upstream rule of thumb is 5 to 10% per field).

**Framing.** State the value plainly ("Get the full 25-page guide free"). Show a preview: contents, first page, sample result. Add proof only if it is true: a download count nobody can back is not proof. Lower the risk: "No spam. Unsubscribe anytime", which the Commander must then honour.

## Page and delivery

Landing page, in order:

1. Headline: what they get and why it matters.
2. A preview of the magnet.
3. What is inside: three to five points.
4. Proof: real numbers, testimonials, logos.
5. The form: minimal fields, a button that says what they get.
6. FAQ: is it really free, what format is it.

| Delivery | For | Against |
|---|---|---|
| Instant download | Immediate | Email not verified |
| By email | Verifies the address, starts the relationship | Short delay |
| Thank-you page plus email | Both | Slightly more to set up |
| Dripped over days | Builds a habit | Only for courses and series |

The thank-you page should confirm delivery ("check your inbox"), offer one next step (book a demo, start a trial, join the community), and point to related content.

## Promotion

- **On the blog:** calls to action inside and at the end of related posts. A content upgrade made for one post converts far better than a generic sidebar box.
- **Exit and scroll prompts:** match the offer to the page being read. Skip unless the site has a popup tool.
- **Social:** share excerpts; turn key points into a multi-image post; put the magnet in the profile link.
- **Paid:** social lead ads for early-stage magnets, search ads for high-intent ones (templates, tools), LinkedIn for business audiences, retargeting for blog visitors. Paid spend is the Commander's decision; propose a budget, never commit one.
- **Partners:** cross-promotion with complementary brands, guest webinars, partner newsletters, shared resource collections.

## Measurement

| Metric | What it tells you | Rough range |
|---|---|---|
| Landing page sign-up rate | How attractive the offer is | 20 to 40% warm traffic, 5 to 15% cold |
| Cost per lead | Efficiency | Varies by channel |
| Lead-to-customer rate | Lead quality | 1 to 5% in business markets |
| Email engagement after delivery | Relevance | 30 to 50% open, 2 to 5% click |
| Time to first purchase | How well the follow-up works | Track per magnet |

More ranges by format, channel and cost are in `references/benchmarks.md` (included at the end of this skill). They are general guides from the upstream author. The Commander's own numbers replace them as soon as there are any.

**Good leads look like:** above-average email engagement, progress to trial or demo at the expected rate, few unsubscribes after delivery, a match with the ideal customer.

**First tests:** headline (benefit against curiosity); format (checklist against guide on the same topic); gate (full against preview); fields (email only against email and name); button wording; instant download against email delivery.

## Rules

- **Plan and draft; do not publish.** Do not create forms, pages, popups or ad campaigns, and do not spend. The Commander sets those up.
- **Nothing untrue on the page.** No invented download counts, testimonials or results. Every promise in the title is kept inside.
- **Ranges are rough.** Label benchmark figures as general guides, never as a forecast for this business.
- **Collect the minimum.** Rules on email sign-ups differ by country; note that meeting them is the Commander's responsibility and do not give legal advice.
- **One format per magnet.** Do not mix a guide, a video and a spreadsheet.

## Output

A plan at `lead-magnets/<topic>-plan.md`:

1. **Recommendation:** topic, format, buyer stage, why this format for this audience, effort estimate.
2. **Outline:** title options, sections with key points, length, what makes it valuable, the closing next step.
3. **Exchange and capture:** what is gated, form fields, landing page structure, delivery method, thank-you page.
4. **Distribution:** channels in order, content upgrade opportunities, paid options with a proposed budget if relevant.
5. **Measurement:** metrics with targets, the first test.
6. **Open questions:** existing content to reuse, where the audience spends time, whether a follow-up email sequence exists, budget for design and promotion.

## Done means

The plan is saved and re-read; it names one problem, one format and one buyer stage; the outline is complete enough to build from; every number is either the Commander's or labelled a rough guide; and nothing was published or spent.
