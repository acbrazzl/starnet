# Benchmarks and common mistakes

**Read this first.** The figures below are the ones the upstream skill reports from vendor studies dated 2024 to 2025 (Belkins, Snov.io, Reachoutly, Digital Bloom, Instantly, Lavender, Gong, Woodpecker). StarNet has not re-checked them and they age quickly. Use them to choose between options and to set rough expectations. Do not quote them to a prospect, and when you show one to the Commander, say where it comes from.

## Rough performance ranges

| Measure | Average | Good | Excellent |
|---|---|---|---|
| Open rate | about 28% | 40 to 45% | 50% and up |
| Reply rate | 4 to 6% | 5 to 10% | 10 to 15% |
| Share of replies that are positive | about 48% | 55 to 60% | over 60% |
| Meetings booked per email sent | 0.5 to 1% | 1 to 2% | over 2% |
| Bounce rate | 7.5% | under 4% | under 2% |

A realistic funnel for an average sender: 500 emails, 25 replies, 8 positive, 4 meetings, 1 customer. Reply rates have been falling year on year as inboxes fill, which makes the writing matter more.

## What moves results, strongest first

1. The hook. Openings tied to a timely event beat generic problem statements.
2. Depth of personalisation.
3. Brevity. 25 to 75 words is the range to aim for.
4. Tight targeting. Small, well-chosen batches (about 50 contacts or fewer) beat large ones.
5. Following up at all.
6. Simple language. Short words and short sentences.
7. Timing. Midweek tends to do best.

## Fifteen mistakes, by damage done

1. Too long.
2. Too much "we" and "I".
3. No clear value for the reader.
4. A generic template. A first name is not personalisation.
5. Listing features. One proof point beats ten.
6. False personalisation: praise with no specifics.
7. Asking for too much in the first email.
8. Pushy language.
9. No ask at all.
10. "Just checking in" follow-ups.
11. The wrong tone for the reader's role.
12. Jargon and buzzwords.
13. Claims with no proof.
14. Writing to too many people at the same company. One or two is the limit.
15. Fake urgency, fake "Re:".

## Who replies

Senior people answer less often but are worth more when they do; they reward brevity. Heads of sales are the most written-to role and the most tired of it. Response varies widely by industry, and crowded fields such as software and financial services sit at the low end.

## Tone by region

| | United States | United Kingdom | Germany, Austria, Switzerland | Nordic countries |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tone | Direct, casual | Polite, professional | Precise, data-led | Factual, egalitarian |
| Length | Short, blunt | Longer, insight-led | Detailed | Concise but substantial |
| Proof | Outcome numbers | Research-led credibility | Technical precision | Shared values |

Europe has stricter rules on unsolicited email than the United States. Flag this to the Commander for European prospects; do not advise on the law.

## A fast personalisation routine

From the "30 Minutes to President's Club" method: list the ten buying signals that matter most (five about the company, five about the person) and rank them. Write one paragraph per signal that opens with the signal and bridges into the problem you solve. Keep the rest of the email constant. A prospect who shows one of the signals gets that paragraph, filled with the real detail from your source table. A prospect who shows none gets the role-level version.
