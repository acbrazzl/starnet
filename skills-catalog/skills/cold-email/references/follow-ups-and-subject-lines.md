# Follow-ups and subject lines

You write the sequence; the Commander sends it. Give each email its day gap so the Commander can schedule it in their own tool.

## How many

Three to five emails in total. Most replies to cold outreach come from follow-ups, yet returns fall and complaints rise after the fourth follow-up. Cap the sequence at four follow-ups and make each one add something new.

## Cadence

Widen the gap each time.

| Email | Day | Note |
|---|---|---|
| First email | 0 | Most of the personalisation effort goes here |
| Follow-up 1 | 3 | A different angle |
| Follow-up 2 | 7 to 8 | Proof from a similar company |
| Follow-up 3 | 14 | A new insight or resource |
| Follow-up 4 | 21 to 28 | The breakup email |

Suggest midweek sends (Tuesday to Thursday), mid-morning or early afternoon in the prospect's time zone. Avoid Monday morning and Friday afternoon. These are rules of thumb; the Commander's own results outrank them.

## Angle rotation

Each email must stand on its own and still move toward the goal. One new value point per email.

| Email | Angle | Purpose |
|---|---|---|
| First | Personal hook, core value, soft ask | Introduce the problem and the solution |
| Follow-up 1 | A new value point: a number, an insight, a resource | Show another benefit |
| Follow-up 2 | Proof or a case from a similar company (real, from the Commander) | Build credibility |
| Follow-up 3 | A trend or resource relevant to their role | Show expertise |
| Follow-up 4 | Breakup: acknowledge the silence, leave the door open | Close the loop |

## The breakup email

Structure: acknowledge you have written several times; accept that it may not be relevant; say this is the last email for now; leave the door open.

> I haven't heard back, so I'll assume now isn't the right time. Before I close the loop: [one-sentence insight or resource]. If that changes things, just reply. Otherwise no hard feelings, and good luck with [their goal].

The 1-2-3 form makes replying almost effortless:

> Since I haven't heard back, I'll keep it simple. Reply with a number:
> 1. Interested, let's talk
> 2. Not now, check back in three months
> 3. Not interested, please stop

**If a breakup email goes out, it is honoured.** Tell the Commander not to contact that prospect again unless they reply.

## Phrases that hurt replies

- "I never heard back"
- "Just checking in"
- "Bumping this to the top of your inbox"
- "Did you see my last email?"
- "Following up on my previous message"

All of them add nothing and put the burden on the reader.

## The ask, by seniority

- Executives and founders: tiny effort, curiosity-led. "Curious?" "Worth two minutes?"
- Mid-level managers: more specific. "Want me to walk through how [company] did [result]?"

The higher the reader sits, the less you can ask of them.

## Subject lines

The counter-intuitive finding across the vendor studies upstream draws on: short, plain, internal-looking subject lines win.

- **Length:** two to four words. Phones cut subjects off at about 30 to 35 characters.
- **Internal look:** it should resemble a note from a colleague. Examples: "reply rates", "trial delays", "hiring ops", "employee turnover", "q2 forecast", "second page".
- **Lowercase** reads as more personal than title case.
- **Context, not name:** refer to a pain, a competitor, a trigger event or a shared connection. A first name in the subject line signals automation.
- **Questions** only when specific to a pain ("help with [challenge]?"). Generic ones ("quick question?", "have 15 minutes?") fail. Default to a plain statement.

Avoid:

| Pattern | Why |
|---|---|
| Sales words ("increase", "boost", "ROI") | Looks like a pitch before it is opened |
| Urgency ("ASAP", "urgent") | Reads as pressure |
| Heavy punctuation | Reads as spam |
| Numbers and percentages | Reads as an advert |
| Emoji | Undercuts a business email |
| The product pitched in the subject | Removes the reason to open |
| Spam triggers ("free", "guarantee", "act now") | Delivery risk |
| Fake "Re:" or "Fwd:" | Dishonest; destroys trust |

For senior executives keep it very short and human: the name of their initiative, "a question", or a reference to the specific project or event you read about.
