# Test ideas by page type

A/B test ideas to offer when a change is a judgment call. Each line is a test and the question it answers. Pick the few that match your findings; do not hand over the whole list. Running a split test needs a testing tool and enough traffic on the Commander's side: you propose the hypothesis, the variants and what to measure, and the Commander runs it.

Write each proposal as: "Because [what you saw], we believe [change] will [effect] for [visitors]. Measure [metric]."

## Homepage

**First screen**

- Headline: specific against abstract wording.
- Sub-headline: add or sharpen it to support the headline.
- Main button on the first screen: present or absent.
- Main visual: product screenshot, short animation, illustration or video.
- Button wording: "Start free trial", "Get started", "See demo".
- Button contrast and size.
- An interactive demo in place of a static image.

**Trust and proof**

- Customer logos on the first screen against further down.
- A case result in the first screen.
- Proof in the headline (a real customer count).
- Testimonials near the top against in their own section.
- Security, compliance or award badges.

**Content**

- Icons with short descriptions against fuller feature sections.
- Section order: highest-value content moved up.
- Outcomes first against features first.
- A comparison section against competitors or the old way.
- More or fewer secondary buttons down the page.

**Navigation**

- Navigation that stays in view with the main button in it.
- Order of menu items.
- A chat widget: its effect on the main action.
- Footer tidied toward the secondary actions.

## Pricing page

- Annual against monthly shown by default; savings highlighted.
- A "most popular" marker on the target plan.
- Number of visible tiers: two, three or four.
- Plan order, to anchor expectations.
- Enterprise tier with a starting price against "Contact sales".
- Comparison table against expandable sections.
- A calculator for usage-based pricing.
- A short quiz that recommends a plan.
- An FAQ for pricing objections.
- A guarantee placed prominently.
- Per-user breakdowns for team plans.
- Testimonials about value and return near the prices.

## Demo request page

- Fewer fields.
- Multi-step with a progress bar against one step.
- Form high on the page against after the content.
- Phone field present or absent.
- Benefits restated above the form.
- A preview of what the demo covers.
- "What you will learn" to set expectations.
- Testimonials beside the form.
- Button wording: "Book your demo" against "Schedule a 15-minute call".
- An on-demand recorded demo offered beside the live one.
- A booking calendar on the page against a link elsewhere.
- Site navigation removed.

## Blog and resource pages

- A call to action that stays in view against one at the end only.
- Inline calls to action at natural stopping points.
- A content upgrade specific to the article.
- Related resources at the end.
- Gated against free for a given resource.
- Filters and search in the resource library.

## Landing pages

**Message match**

- Headline that repeats the ad's wording.
- Visual that repeats the ad's visual.
- The same offer the ad promised.
- A separate page per audience segment.

**Focus**

- Navigation removed.
- The main button repeated down the page.
- A form on the page against a button that leads to one.
- Amount and placement of proof.
- A video explaining the offer.
- Urgency or limited availability, only when it is real.

**Length**

- Short against long.
- Most important content first.
- Footer removed.

## Feature pages

- A demo or screenshot of the feature in use.
- Use-case examples.
- Before and after.
- A video walkthrough.
- A trial offer specific to the feature.
- Links to related features, the matching plan, and a customer story about this feature.

## Forms

- Single step against multi-step.
- One column against two.
- Required against optional balance.
- Labels above the field against inside it.
- Privacy line and proof beside the button.
- Button wording.
- Auto-focus on the first field.
- Conditional fields that appear based on earlier answers.

## Across the site

- Consent banner design that covers less of the page.
- Page speed against heavy features.
- Phone layout improvements.
- Breadcrumbs and search placement.
- A call to action always present in the navigation.

## Before proposing a test

- One change per test, so the result can be read.
- The page needs enough visitors for a result to mean anything. If traffic is low, recommend making the obvious fixes directly and saving tests for close calls.
- Name the metric before the test starts.
